Coefficient-strength agreement

Rank correlation of the 870 off-diagonal |Phiij| vs |alphaij| values. Near 0 means the models do not rank the same directed pairs as strong lagged links.

Spearman ρ · dates with no Chen variation (empty or one-valued alpha) are omitted.

Interpretation Spearman ρ ranks the 870 off-diagonal |Φij| against |αij|. Its purpose is to test whether LS-LR-NVAR and Chen put the same directed pairs at the top of the lagged-strength list, regardless of coefficient scale.

They do not. Versus Chen direct the correlation stays near zero (mean −0.009): slightly positive in 2019–2020 (at most +0.05) and negative after 2024 (about −0.07 at the origin). Versus Chen factor-adjusted it also stays inside ±0.08, with gaps when the residual graph is empty. Neither Chen spec ranks lagged pairs the way LS-LR-NVAR does.

Kendall τ of |Phi| vs |alpha|

Same pairs as the Spearman panel; more robust to a few large coefficients.

Interpretation Kendall τ is the same pairwise ranking test as Spearman, but it is less sensitive to a few very large coefficients. Its purpose is to check that any agreement (or disagreement) is not driven by LASSO outliers.

LS-LR-NVAR and Chen still disagree. Versus direct LASSO the mean is −0.007 and the path tracks Spearman; versus factor-adjusted it is +0.004. Down-weighting large α does not bring the two models’ lag orderings together.

Normalized Frobenius distance

Scale-free distance of strength matrices. Smaller is closer. A random direction is around 1.4.

Interpretation Normalized Frobenius is ||Ŝours − Ŝchen|| after each strength matrix is scaled to unit length. Its purpose is to measure how close the two lag-strength patterns are once units are removed (0 = the same direction, ≈1.41 = orthogonal).

LS-LR-NVAR’s S and Chen’s S stay far apart: 1.20–1.38 versus direct (mean 1.31) and mean 1.33 versus factor-adjusted. The only modest dip is 2019–2020, when Chen is densest; even then the operators do not point the same way. After scale is stripped, the two models’ strength patterns are nearly orthogonal.

Companion spectral radius

Largest absolute eigenvalue of the VAR(2) companion built from (Phi1, Phi2) vs (alpha1, alpha2). This is a property of the operator, not of the named network drawing.

LS-LR-NVAR last-origin draws are capped at 0.92 in the forecast program; the path here is the unrestricted posterior-mean operator at each date.

Interpretation The companion spectral radius is the largest absolute eigenvalue of the VAR(2) companion built from (Φ1, Φ2) or (α1, α2). Its purpose is to compare how persistent each model’s lag operator is — a property of the dynamics, not of the named network drawing.

The two models do not share a persistence path. LS-LR-NVAR stays in a 0.25–0.40 band and only rises to 0.49 at the origin (moderate, stable). Chen direct tracks its own density cycle: near 0.50 in 2020, essentially 0 in 2022 when the Granger graph collapses, then 0.30 by February 2026. Chen factor-adjusted is ~0 on almost every date because residual lags are tiny. LS-LR-NVAR’s operator is steadier; Chen’s shrinks and expands with sparsity.

Protocol

LS-LR-NVAR

Blocked Gibbs, 24 sweeps, 12 burn-in, thin 2 (6 kept draws). Posterior mean of daily Phik,t and At. Evaluated at the training index nearest each Chen tau.

Chen TV-LASSO

Equation-wise LassoCV at tau = 1, then that lambda on 36 local-linear fits (h = 0.20, p = 2). Granger edge if any lag is nonzero. Own lags dropped. Direct: raw returns. Factor-adjusted: PCA residuals.

Overall assessment

Purpose of this section: put the chart-by-chart scores on one footing and say whether LS-LR-NVAR and Chen TV-LASSO recover the same dynamic network for these 30 DJIA names. Direct LASSO is the clean comparison (both estimated on raw returns). Factor-adjusted LASSO is Chen’s preferred spec for correlated panels, but its α is residual Granger after PCA, not a reduced-form match to Φ.

Verdict After mapping both models onto the same objects — lag operators, matched-k edges, influence ranks, and companion persistence — LS-LR-NVAR and Chen do not describe the same network. They are complementary views (contemporaneous association that modulates lags vs sparse time-varying Granger), not two estimates of one graph. Treating our At as Chen’s Granger adjacency, or the reverse, would be a category error.

Where they differ

Lag-strength rankings are uncorrelated: mean Spearman of |Φ| vs |α| is 0.044 versus direct and -0.007 versus factor-adjusted. Normalized Frobenius stays near 1.3 (random is ~1.41), so the strength matrices are nearly orthogonal. Matched-k overlap versus direct is 9.6%, no better than chance (9.8%); versus factor-adjusted it is 1.5%. In-influence is the sharpest split (mean 0.133 vs direct): Chen’s late-sample sources are GS, AXP, JPM; LS-LR-NVAR’s hubs stay HD, CSCO, HON. Persistence also diverges — our companion radius is stable (~0.32, 0.49 at the origin); Chen direct follows its density cycle (≈0.50 in 2020, ≈0 in 2022, 0.30 at the origin); factor-adjusted is essentially 0.

What that implies

LS-LR-NVAR is the model of a dense, almost undirected, slowly moving association network that damps lags through (I+W)⊙B. Chen is the model of a sparse, hub-and-spoke Granger graph that thickens around 2020 and thins in 2022. Factor adjustment removes most of those Granger links, which is why Chen’s paper-recommended spec looks empty next to ours. The 6-month open-loop forecast comparison is consistent with this: LS-LR-NVAR beat Chen factor-adjusted on path MAPE (10.81% vs 12.22%), while Chen direct was slightly tighter (10.18%) — a mean-path ranking, not evidence that the two networks coincide.

Why propose LS-LR-NVAR rather than use Chen et al.

The case for our model is the estimand, not a claim that every sparse VAR is obsolete. Chen, Li, Li and Linton (JoE 2025) estimate a time-varying Granger network by local-linear LASSO. For equity panels they recommend first removing common factors, then shrinking the idiosyncratics. That is the right tool if the object of interest is a few directed lagged links after market factors have been taken out. It is the wrong default if the object is the locally stationary association structure that actually enters a return VAR.

What our model is for

Equity returns are densely and almost symmetrically dependent. A 30-name DJIA panel has a correlation floor that already covers most pairs; a probit network with low-rank latent factors is a natural model of that graph. LS-LR-NVAR puts that graph into the mean: yt is driven by a low-rank local level and by (I+Wt)⊙Bt, with Bt itself low-rank and locally stationary. The network is a parameter of the dynamics, not a post-hoc drawing of nonzero LASSO coefficients. That lets us talk about density, reciprocity, clustering, and slowly moving hubs (HD, CSCO, HON, SHW) as features of the market, rather than as artifacts of a penalty.

Why Chen is not a substitute

On the same DJIA sample, Chen’s factor-adjusted Granger graph has mean density 1.1% and is often empty; the direct LASSO graph cycles from ~24% around 2020 to ~2% in 2022. After we map both models onto Φ versus α, rank correlations sit on zero and matched-k overlap is at or below chance. The two estimators are not two readings of one network. Using Chen in place of LS-LR-NVAR would throw away the contemporaneous, reciprocal structure that our model is written to capture, and — under Chen’s own recommended spec for correlated panels — would also worsen the 6-month open-loop path (MAPE 12.22% and Spearman 0.07 versus 10.81% and 0.19).

How we would state the contribution We recommend LS-LR-NVAR when the aim is to estimate a locally stationary low-rank network VAR for a correlated financial panel: a dense association network, a low-rank local mean, and a low-rank lag loading, with uncertainty from a blocked Gibbs sampler. We do not recommend it as a sparse Granger detector. Chen remaining useful as a complementary check: if the question is which directed lags survive L1 after factors, their Section 5 specification is the benchmark we already beat on this holdout; if the question is raw-return sparsity, their direct LASSO can be slightly tighter on path MAPE (10.18%) and should be reported as a robustness comparator, not as the model of the association network.

Limitations we should keep in the paper: the implemented probit uses a correlation-threshold floor, so reported density on equities is partly inherited from contemporaneous |corr|; on a weak-signal blocked-SBM simulation that floor does not fire and Ât collapses. Gibbs here used a short run (24 sweeps, 6 kept draws). These are implementation and identification caveats, not reasons to prefer Chen’s estimand for the problem we state.
